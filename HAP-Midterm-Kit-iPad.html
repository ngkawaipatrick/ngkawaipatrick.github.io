Week 1 · Cell basics (not on the slides, but in the self-assessment)

Nine of the eighteen Week 1 self-assessment questions are on cells. The slides never cover it, so this section is built from the textbook at the depth those questions use: definitions, stage names, and pairs of opposites. That is all you need.

Cell parts

  • Plasma membrane: phospholipid bilayer that controls what enters and leaves.
  • Cytoplasm: everything between membrane and nucleus. Organelles ("little organs") are the structures inside it.
  • Nucleus holds DNA (chromatin → condenses to chromosomes). Nucleolus makes ribosomes.
  • Ribosomes make protein. Rough ER (with ribosomes) processes protein; smooth ER makes lipids. Golgi packages and ships. Mitochondria make ATP ("powerhouse"). Lysosomes digest ("suicide sacs"). Centrioles organise the spindle in division. Cilia/flagella move things.

DNA → protein (two steps, two words)

  • Transcription: DNA → mRNA, in the nucleus, by RNA polymerase.
  • Translation: mRNA → protein, at the ribosome, using transfer RNA (tRNA) to bring amino acids. "Protein synthesis by ribosomes begins with translation."
  • Complementary base pairing: A–T (A–U in RNA), C–G.

Moving things across the membrane

TermEnergy?Meaning
Passive transportNoUmbrella term: substance moves down its gradient. Includes diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis, filtration.
DiffusionNoHigh → low concentration until even. (Likely key for "movement across a membrane without cell energy".)
Facilitated diffusionNoDiffusion through a carrier or channel protein (glucose).
OsmosisNoDiffusion of water through a selectively permeable membrane.
FiltrationNo (uses pressure)Fluid pushed through a membrane by hydrostatic pressure (kidney).
Active transportYes (ATP)Umbrella term: substance moved against its gradient, e.g. Na⁺/K⁺ pump.
EndocytosisYesMembrane traps material and pulls it in. Phagocytosis = solids ("cell eating"); pinocytosis = fluids/dissolved molecules ("cell drinking").
ExocytosisYesVesicle fuses with membrane and releases contents out.

Mitosis: one word per stage

StageOne-line cue
InterphaseNot dividing; DNA replicates. Longest phase.
ProphaseChromatin condenses into chromosomes; nuclear envelope breaks down; spindle forms.
MetaphaseChromosomes align in the middle (metaphase plate).
AnaphaseChromatids pulled apart / away to opposite poles.
TelophaseNuclear envelopes and nuclei reappear; cytokinesis splits the cytoplasm.

Mnemonic: I-P-M-A-T ("I Passed My Anatomy Test"). Meiosis (for gametes) halves the chromosome number, 46 → 23.

Cell size and fate

  • Hypertrophy = increase in cell size. Hyperplasia = increase in cell number. Atrophy = decrease in size (disuse). Anaplasia = loss of differentiation (cancer). Metaplasia = one cell type replaced by another.
  • Apoptosis = programmed cell death ("cell suicide"). Necrosis = death from injury.
  • Four tissue types: epithelial, connective (includes bone, blood), muscle, nervous.
Week 1 · Terminology, homeostasis, axial skeleton

Orientation

  • Coronal plane → anterior/posterior. Transverse → superior/inferior. Median (mid-sagittal) → equal left/right; sagittal → any left/right cut.
  • Anatomical position: upright, facing forward, palms forward, feet flat and forward. So the forearm is in supination and the elbow in extension.
  • Body cavities: dorsal (cranial cavity + vertebral canal) and ventral (thoracic; abdominopelvic = abdominal + pelvic). Diaphragm separates thoracic from abdominal.
  • 9 abdominal regions: transpyloric line = L1, intertubercular line = L5, mid-clavicular lines. Epigastric / R+L hypochondriac; umbilical / R+L lumbar (flank); pubic (hypogastric) / R+L inguinal (iliac).
  • Proximal = closer to attachment; distal = farther. Superficial/deep. Cephalic/caudal.
  • Negative feedback reverses the stimulus (temperature, pH, O₂). Positive feedback amplifies it: childbirth (oxytocin), ends with an external event.
  • 11 organ systems; anatomy = structure seen without a microscope (gross anatomy).

Skeleton basics

  • Functions (slide list): support, movement, protection, blood formation, electrolyte balance, acid–base balance, detoxification, triglyceride storage. Not heat production (that is muscle).
  • 206 adult bones, ~270 at birth. Axial 80, appendicular 126.
  • Axial 80: cranium 8, face 14, ossicles 6, hyoid 1, C7, T12, L5, sacrum 1, coccyx 1, sternum 1, ribs 24.
  • Shapes: long (humerus), short (carpals), flat (sternum), irregular (vertebra), sesamoid (patella).
  • Long bone: diaphysis (shaft), epiphyses (ends), metaphyses, articular (hyaline) cartilage, periosteum, medullary cavity = yellow marrow.
  • Cells: osteoprogenitor → osteoblast (builds) → osteocyte (mature, maintains). Osteoclast = multinucleated, resorbs bone, from white-cell lineage.
  • Haemopoiesis in red bone marrow: pluripotent stem cell → myeloid and lymphoid lines.

  • Sutures = fibrous, immovable (synarthrosis). Coronal: frontal–parietals. Sagittal: parietal–parietal. Lambdoid (Λ): parietals–occipital. Squamous: temporal–parietal.
  • Parietal forms most of the superior skull; temporal has the external auditory (acoustic) meatus; occipital has the foramen magnum.
  • Fontanelles: anterior is largest, closes 18–24 months; others by ~12 months. Anterior, posterior, sphenoid, mastoid.
  • Paranasal sinuses: frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid, maxillary.
  • Orbit = 7 bones. Roof frontal + sphenoid; floor maxilla + zygomatic + palatine; medial wall maxilla, lacrimal, ethmoid, sphenoid; lateral zygomatic + sphenoid.
  • Hyoid: anterior neck, level of C3, articulates with no other bone, suspended by muscles/ligaments.
  • Facial expression muscles = CN VII, facial nerve.

Vertebral column

  • 33 in development → adult 26: 7 C, 12 T, 5 L, 1 sacrum (5 fused), 1 coccyx (4 fused).
  • Curves: cervical & lumbar convex (anteriorly), thoracic & sacral concave.
  • Parts: body, pedicle, lamina, spinous process, transverse process, vertebral foramen. Opening between adjacent vertebrae for the spinal nerve = intervertebral foramen.
  • Cervical: small, 3 foramina (1 vertebral + 2 transverse), bifid spinous process (C7 not bifid, large). C1 atlas (no body), C2 axis with dens; anterior arch of atlas + dens = median atlanto-axial joint.
  • Thoracic: long spinous processes pointing inferiorly, costal facets for ribs (vertebrocostal joints).
  • Lumbar: largest, short blunt spinous processes pointing posteriorly, thickest discs.
  • Facet joints between articular processes of adjacent vertebrae = apophyseal (zygapophyseal) joints, synovial.
  • Disorders: scoliosis lateral; kyphosis thoracic (hunchback); lordosis lumbar. Herniated disc: nucleus pulposus through annulus fibrosus, most common lumbar. Spina bifida: laminae of L5/S1 fail to close.

Thorax & joints

  • Sternum: manubrium, body, xiphoid process; sternal angle at rib 2. Rib–sternum = sternocostal joint (via costal cartilage).
  • Rib: head, neck, tubercle, body, costal groove carrying V-A-N (vein, artery, nerve, top to bottom).
  • Structural: fibrous (sutures), cartilaginous (pubic symphysis), synovial (finger joint, cavity + capsule). Functional: synarthrosis immovable, amphiarthrosis slightly, diarthrosis freely.

Lower limb landmarks

  • Pelvis: iliac crest, ASIS/AIIS, ischial tuberosity, pubic symphysis (cartilaginous), acetabulum (its lunate surface articulates with head of femur = hip joint), obturator foramen, sacroiliac joint (auricular surfaces). True pelvis below pelvic brim.

Bone formation & repair

  • Intramembranous ossification: flat skull bones, mandible (4 steps: ossification centre, calcification, trabeculae, periosteum).
  • Endochondral: cartilage model → primary ossification centre → medullary cavity → secondary centre → articular cartilage + epiphyseal plate. Most bones, all long bones.
  • Epiphyseal plate zones: resting → proliferating → hypertrophic → calcified cartilage. Growth in thickness by periosteal osteoblasts (circumferential lamellae).
  • Fracture repair: reactive (haematoma) → reparative (fibrocartilaginous callus → bony callus) → remodeling.
  • Fractures: greenstick (children, incomplete), comminuted (fragments), impacted, open, Colles (distal radius), Pott (ankle/fibula).
  • Osteoporosis: resorption > formation; more common in women.
  • PTH ↑ blood Ca²⁺ (↑ resorption, ↓ renal loss). Calcitonin ↓ blood Ca²⁺ (↓ osteoclasts). cAMP messenger.
  • Limbs from mesoderm, limb buds weeks 4–8, then endochondral ossification.

Muscle & movement

  • >600 skeletal muscles. Functions include heat production, stability, glucose regulation. Origin = stationary bone; insertion = movable bone.
  • Lever: bone = lever, joint = fulcrum, muscle = effort.
  • Movements: gliding; angular (flexion/extension/hyperextension, abduction/adduction, circumduction); rotation (medial/lateral); special (protraction/retraction at TMJ, pronation/supination).
  • Motor unit: more fibres per unit → less precise control.
  • Integument: Rule of 9s (adult: head 9, each arm 9, anterior trunk 18, posterior trunk 18, each leg 18, perineum 1). Vitamin D₃ in epidermis.
Week 3 · Respiratory system

Anatomy

  • Conducting division (airflow) vs respiratory division (gas exchange). URT = nostrils to larynx; LRT = trachea to lungs.
  • Functions: gas exchange, communication, olfaction, acid–base balance, BP regulation (angiotensin II production).
  • Nose warms, filters, humidifies. Pharyngeal tonsil in nasopharynx.
  • Larynx: ~4 cm, 9 cartilages: epiglottis 1, thyroid 1 (largest, Adam's apple), cricoid 1, arytenoid 2, corniculate 2, cuneiform 2. Vocal cords adduct via lateral cricoarytenoid (sound), abduct via posterior cricoarytenoid (breathing).
  • Trachea: 16–20 C-shaped hyaline rings, open side posterior (oesophagus); mucus + cilia escalator; bifurcates at T4/5 (carina) into main bronchi.
  • Airway generations: trachea 0 → main bronchi 1 → … → alveolar sacs 23. ~300 million alveoli.
  • Alveolus: Type I squamous = gas exchange; Type II great = surfactant + repair; alveolar macrophages. Respiratory membrane = alveolar epithelium + fused basement membrane + capillary endothelium.
  • Right lung 3 lobes, 2 fissures (oblique, horizontal); left 2 lobes, 1 fissure, cardiac notch. Hilum/root; apex above clavicle; base on diaphragm.
  • Pleura: visceral (on lung) inner, parietal (chest wall) outer; pleural fluid.
  • Development: pulmonary groove ~3 wk, lung buds wk 4, diaphragm wk 8–9, fetal breathing ~11 wk, alveoli after 6 months, adult number by age 8–10.

Ventilation (physics you already know)

  • Boyle: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. Dalton: partial pressures add. Henry: dissolved gas ∝ partial pressure × solubility.
  • Inspiration: diaphragm contracts and descends, thoracic volume ↑, alveolar pressure ~758 mmHg (atm 760) → air in. Expiration: relax, ~762 → air out.
  • Intrapleural pressure always negative (≈ −4 mmHg; −5 to −8 cmH₂O), from lung recoil inward vs chest wall recoil outward. Transpulmonary P = Palveolar − Pintrapleural.
  • Other factors: surface tension (reduced by surfactant), compliance, airway resistance.
  • Volumes: tidal ~0.5 L, IRV, ERV, residual volume (never expelled). Capacities: inspiratory capacity, FRC (air left after quiet expiration), vital capacity (max in/out in one cycle), total lung capacity.

Gas transport & control

  • Three phases: pulmonary ventilation → external respiration (lung) → internal respiration (tissue).
  • Shunt flow ~2% → arterial PO₂ ~95 mmHg. Lungs PO₂ ~100 (Hb ~100% saturated); tissues 20–40.
  • O₂: >98% on haemoglobin (4 hemes, Fe), <2% dissolved.
  • CO₂: 70% bicarbonate, 23% carbaminohaemoglobin, 7% dissolved. Carbonic anhydrase in RBC; chloride shift (Cl⁻ in at tissues, out at lungs).
  • Bohr effect: ↑CO₂/↑H⁺ shifts the curve right → more O₂ unloaded. BPG (hypoxia) also right shift.
  • Control: medulla oblongata DRG (inspiration) + VRG (forced); pons pneumotaxic centre inhibits apneustic centre. Motor output via phrenic nerve. Central chemoreceptors in medulla sense CSF pH; peripheral in carotid bodies & aortic arch sense O₂, CO₂, pH, signal via CN IX and X. Stretch receptors → CN X → prevent over-inflation. Irritant receptors → cough.
Week 4 · Cardiovascular system

Anatomy

  • Heart in the middle mediastinum; oesophagus and descending aorta in the posterior mediastinum. Apex toward left, PMI ~5th intercostal space.
  • Pericardium: fibrous (outer) → parietal serous → pericardial cavity → visceral serous (epicardium). Wall: epicardium, myocardium, endocardium.
  • Aortic arch branches: brachiocephalic, left common carotid, left subclavian. Ligamentum arteriosum = remnant of ductus arteriosus.
  • Valves: right AV = tricuspid; left AV = bicuspid/mitral; semilunar = pulmonary and aortic. AV valves anchored by chordae tendineae to papillary muscles. Fibrous skeleton holds valve rings.
  • S1 "lub" = AV valves close (start of ventricular systole). S2 "dub" = semilunar valves close. S3 early diastole (normal in children, volume overload in adults); S4 late diastole, stiff ventricle.
  • Left ventricle wall is thickest (systemic pressure).
  • Lungs: 2 pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood; 4 pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood to the left atrium. Bronchial arteries feed lung tissue; bronchial veins → right atrium.
  • Blood path: SVC/IVC/coronary sinus → RA → tricuspid → RV → pulmonary valve → pulmonary trunk → lungs → pulmonary veins → LA → mitral → LV → aortic valve → aorta.
  • Coronary arteries from aortic root. LCA → anterior interventricular (LAD) + circumflex (→ left marginal). RCA → right marginal + posterior interventricular. Veins: great, middle, small, anterior cardiac → coronary sinus → right atrium. Anastomoses = collateral circulation.
  • Fetal shunts: foramen ovale (RA → LA) and ductus arteriosus (pulmonary trunk → aorta); close at first breath.
  • Cardiac muscle: branched, one nucleus, striated, involuntary, intercalated discs (gap junctions + desmosomes).

Electrical

  • Conducting order: SA node (pacemaker, RA) → AV node → bundle of His (AV bundle) → bundle branches → Purkinje fibres.
  • Pacemaker potential: no stable resting potential; at −60 mV "funny" Na⁺ channels open (slow ramp) → threshold → Ca²⁺ influx (spike) → K⁺ efflux (repolarise). Cycle 0.8 s → 75 bpm.
  • Contractile fibre: fast Na⁺ depolarisation → plateau (Ca²⁺ in, K⁺ out) → K⁺ repolarisation; ~0.3 s; long refractory period.
  • ECG: P = atrial depolarisation; QRS = ventricular depolarisation (atrial repolarisation hidden); T = ventricular repolarisation. PR interval 0.16 s; ST segment = plateau; QT = whole ventricular depol + repol; RR interval → heart rate.

Mechanical (Wiggers)

  • Atrial systole tops up ventricle (~20%) → EDV. Mitral closes (S1) → isovolumetric contraction (all valves closed, volume flat). Aortic valve opens when LV > 80 mmHg → ejection, peak 120 mmHg. Aortic valve closes (S2, dicrotic notch) → isovolumetric relaxation → mitral opens → rapid filling → diastasis. Lowest volume = ESV.
  • CO = SV × HR. SV = EDV − ESV ≈ 70 mL. 70 × 72 = 5,040 mL/min ≈ 5 L/min.
  • Regulation: cardiovascular centre in medulla oblongata; adrenaline ↑HR; ↑venous return → ↑SV (Frank–Starling).

Vessels & blood pressure

  • Wall: tunica intima (endothelium), media (smooth muscle, thick in arteries), externa. Veins have valves, thin walls. Arteries → arterioles → capillaries (precapillary sphincters) → venules → veins.
  • Pressure highest in large arteries, sharpest drop in arterioles. Total cross-sectional area peaks and velocity is slowest in capillaries.
  • Flow distribution depends on pressure difference and vascular resistance (lumen size, viscosity, length). Skeletal muscle pump + venous valves return blood.
  • RAAS: ↓BP → juxtaglomerular cells (kidney) secrete renin → angiotensinogen (liver) → angiotensin I → ACE (lungs) → angiotensin II → vasoconstriction + aldosterone (adrenal cortex) → Na⁺/water reabsorption (K⁺/H⁺ excreted). High K⁺ also triggers aldosterone.
  • Epinephrine/norepinephrine ↑HR, vasoconstriction (except skeletal muscle). ADH water retention + vasoconstriction. ANP (atria) vasodilation, ↓ blood volume.
Week 5 · Practical applications: respiratory & cardiovascular pathology

COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)

  • Airflow limitation from airway/alveolar damage by noxious substances (smoking, dusts, pollutants, fumes). Two forms: emphysema and chronic bronchitis.
  • Emphysema: irreversible enlargement of airspaces distal to the terminal bronchiole + wall destruction, no fibrosis. Centriacinar / panacinar by location. Loss of elastic recoil → air trapping → ↑ total lung capacity, ↑ residual volume, ↓ FEV₁. Alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency is the genetic cause. Features: dyspnoea, cough with little sputum, pursed-lip breathing ("pink puffer"), weight loss, barrel chest, hypoxaemia. Chest film: hyperinflated elongated lungs, flattened hemidiaphragms, ↑ interstitial markings.
  • Chronic bronchitis: productive cough > 3 months in > 2 consecutive years. Hypertrophy of bronchial mucous glands, goblet-cell hyperplasia, mucus plugs. Cyanosis ("blue bloater"): mucus traps CO₂ → ↑ PCO₂, ↓ PO₂.

Asthma

  • Chronic inflammatory airway disease: reversible bronchoconstriction, mucus hypersecretion, bronchial hyper-responsiveness.
  • Cascade: dendritic cell presents allergen to Th2 cell → (i) Triggering: IL-4 → B cells make IgE → binds mast cells; IL-5 + eotaxin recruit eosinophils. (ii) Immediate (minutes): mast-cell degranulation → oedema, smooth-muscle contraction; vagal reflex adds bronchoconstriction and mucus. (iii) Late (hours): eosinophils, basophils, neutrophils, Th2 infiltrate; eosinophil major basic protein / cationic protein shed the epithelium.
  • Triggers: allergens, viral URTI, cold/dry air, smoke, exercise, stress, aspirin/NSAIDs, beta-blockers. Features: dyspnoea, wheezing, cough with spiral mucus plugs; bronchial mucus cast in status asthmaticus.

Other lung conditions

  • Pneumothorax: air in the pleural space. Spontaneous = ruptured emphysematous bleb; traumatic = penetrating injury. → atelectasis (lung collapse) and mediastinal shift. Treated with a chest tube.
  • Pneumonia: lung infection; diagnosis by chest X-ray, sputum Gram stain/culture, blood culture. Types: lobar, broncho-, interstitial.
  • Tuberculosis (Mycobacterium tuberculosis, inhaled). Primary: caseating necrosis → fibrosis + calcification → Ghon complex. Secondary: reactivation when host resistance falls, at the apex (poor lymphatic drainage, high O₂ tension) → miliary or extrapulmonary TB.
  • Lung cancer: 85% smokers (>60 carcinogens); asbestos, silica. TNM staging (Tumour size, lymph Node spread, Metastasis). Types: adenocarcinoma 50%, squamous 20%, small cell 15%, large cell 2%, other 13%.

Congenital shunts

  • Left-to-right (no cyanosis at first): atrial septal defect, ventricular septal defect, patent ductus arteriosus (aorta ↔ pulmonary trunk stays open). → pulmonary vessel hypertrophy and pulmonary hypertension.
  • Right-to-left (cyanosis): Tetralogy of Fallot = RV outflow stenosis + RV hypertrophy + VSD + overriding aorta; dextro-transposition of the great arteries.

Vessels

  • Atherosclerosis: intimal plaque with necrotic lipid core + fibromuscular cap (calcifies). Sequence: endothelial injury (smoking, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia) → LDL enters intima and is oxidised → monocytes recruited → macrophages engulf lipid → foam cells → cytokines → smooth-muscle cells migrate from media and proliferate → collagen fibrous cap. Risk: hypertension, hypercholesterolaemia, age, male.
  • Aortic aneurysm: localised dilation from wall weakness; thoracic / abdominal / thoracoabdominal.
  • Varicose veins: dilated tortuous veins from ↑ intraluminal pressure + weakened valves (standing, obesity, pregnancy, DVT); stretching → pooling → more stretching. Treat: compression stockings, less standing, sclerotherapy.

Heart

  • Ischaemic heart disease: ↓ myocardial blood supply, mainly coronary atherosclerosis → angina (chest pain) and myocardial infarction (myocyte necrosis). Transmural infarct = permanent occlusion of one branch (LAD, circumflex, RCA). Regional subendocardial = transient/partial obstruction or reperfusion; circumferential subendocardial = global hypotension (shock); microinfarcts = tiny intramural vessels (emboli, vasculitis, spasm).
  • Arrhythmias: tachycardia > 100, bradycardia < 60 bpm. Heart block = delay at AV node / bundle of His (1st, 2nd, complete). Atrial fibrillation 300–600/min, leading risk factor for ischaemic stroke. Ventricular fibrillation = chaotic quivering, no output = cardiac arrest, unconscious in seconds. ECG: AV block = long/dropped PR; AF = irregular, no P waves; VF = chaotic baseline.
  • Congestive heart failure: pump failure → stagnant blood → oedema, ascites. Left-sided (ischaemia, MI, hypertension) → pulmonary oedema; ↓ renal flow → ↑ RAAS → fluid retention. Right-sided (left-to-right shunt, chronic lung disease) → systemic venous congestion: distended neck veins, leg/liver/abdominal oedema.
